Chapter 14: Hol to Chol (The Covenant Split)

Core Objective

To define the definitive, structural split between those integrated into Babylon’s eternal demise and those protected by keeping their precise covenant parameters. This final chapter demonstrates that Babylon and its inhabitants are defined by an absolute, systemic refusal to do God's Commandments, a rebellion that seals their exposure to the auto-executing laws of physics as the Holy Spirit transforms from water to fire.

The closing sequence of Revelation establishes an absolute, binary divide between the total systemic permanent destruction of the Babylonian world order and the preservation of those operating under their divine directives:

The Prophecy
"Blessed are those who do His commandments, that they may have the right to the tree of life, and may enter through the gates into the city" — Revelation 22:14
"Outside are the dogs, the sorcerers, the sexually immoral, the murderers, the idolaters, and everyone who loves and practices falsehood."— Revelation 22:15


In the original Koine Greek text, this insulation is not defined by passive belief, but by precise, actionable parameters:

Οἱ ποιοῦντες τὰς ἐντολὰς αὐτοῦ (Hoi poiountes tas entolas autou): This translates to "the ones actively executing His specific directives/injunctions." The text uses entolai—which denotes precise covenant requirements — rather than nomos (general legalistic code).

The Babylonian Antithesis: By stark and deliberate contrast, Babylon and the Babylonians specifically do NOT do His Commandments. Their entire infrastructure is built on the absolute rejection of the entolai. They are defined by Ἀνομία (Anomia)—active, structural lawlessness and direct hostility to divine Commands. They substitute God's instructions with ta erga tōn cheirōn (the works of their own hands), ensuring they have no right to the tree of life and are left entirely Ἔξω (Exō)—outside, structurally excluded, and exposed.

What is a Saint?

The Meaning of Sanctification
The foundational definition of a saint does not rely on human accolades, institutional decree, or church canonization. The word itself stems from the concepts of being sanctified and holy—completely set apart for the exclusive purpose and ownership of God. A saint is not defined by moral perfection, but by divine ownership.

The Sovereign Census
Because sainthood is a matter of divine ownership, God alone decides who His own saints are, and no one else. Human organizations that attempt to catalog, count, or officially decree who belongs in this category are overstepping their bounds. You cannot take a census of God's elect. To do so mimics the prideful downfalls of worldly empires that attempt to measure what belongs solely to the Divine.

The Revelatory Standard & The Patience of Jesus
For your readers, the ultimate operational standard of a saint is anchored in Revelation 14:12. This verse serves as the definitive structural blueprint: those who possess hupomone (patient endurance) and keep the commandments.

Crucially, the text demands that they maintain the "faith and patience of Jesus." The ultimate historical blueprint for this patience is found in Jesus’ 40 days of endurance in the desert (Matthew 4:1–11). Isolated, starving, and pushed to the absolute limits of physical human endurance, Jesus did not falter. When Satan offered Him all the kingdoms of the world in exchange for a single act of compromise, Jesus relied strictly on the boundaries of God's word to rebuke him. A saint is explicitly identified by this exact same battle-tested endurance—the stubborn capability to stay under the crushing weight of worldly testing without shifting an inch.

The Mechanics of the Clenched Fist Trap

The metaphor of the clenched fist trap illustrates the tragic illusion of human power: like a captive creature reaching into a narrow jar for bait, the trap only works because the captive fiercely refuses to drop what they are holding. In trying to forcibly grip power, wealth, or self-preservation, rebellious humanity guarantees its own destruction.

The Illusion of Violent Seizure & Fraudulent Assemblies
This trap is mechanically sprung when illegitimate groups attempt to bypass God's sovereign authority. This is exposed by the linguistic mechanics of the two primary verses regarding the kingdom and force:

  1. Matthew 11:12: "The kingdom of heaven has been subjected to violence, and violent people have been raiding it."
  2. Luke 16:16: "...and everyone is forcing their way into it."

The Greek root here is biazō—to use force, strength, or violent impetuosity. While rebellious groups think that the Kingdom of God can be seized, managed, or manipulated through sheer human power, political force, or institutional strength, they are caught in the trap. This reality strictly prevents any unapproved assemblies from successfully seizing the kingdom of God.

A primary de facto example of such a fraudulent assembly is the "Synagogue of Satan." As explicitly stated in Revelation 3:9, these are individuals “who say they are Jews but are not and are liars.” They claim a legitimate religious identity, boast of a covenant status, and masquerade as the chosen people of God—but their actions expose the total fraud. They are liars because their posture of righteousness is completely undone by their systemic wickedness. They rely on biazō—worldly manipulation, power, and deceptive religious posture—to grab a spiritual reality, unaware that their clenched fists lock them directly into divine judgment.

The Forfeiture of Covenant and Mercy
The trap tightens completely on the ethical failure of the assembly. The foundational laws of the covenant dictate that the true measure of an assembly's alignment with God is found in how they treat the most vulnerable: the orphan and the widow.

The fraudulent assembly's mask is ripped away because they actively exploit an orphan in direct violation of God's Commandments.

  • The Absolute Bar: You cannot, under any circumstance, claim to be abiding by your covenant if you are actively afflicting, mistreating, or taking advantage of an orphan.

Their clenched fist refuses to let go of the sin of oppression, making it impossible to open their hand to receive mercy.
