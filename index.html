Global Warming and the Destruction of Babylon

Then a mighty angel picked up a stone the size of a great millstone and cast it into the sea, saying: “With such violence the great city of Babylon will be cast down, never to be seen again. 22And the sound of harpists and musicians, of flute players and trumpeters, will never ring out in you again. Nor will any craftsmen of any trade be found in you again, nor the sound of a millstone be heard in you again. — Revelation 18:21-22

John described something “like” a great millstone being thrown into the sea. The word like simply means similar to, not identical. In John’s world, the largest object he could compare to was a millstone — a heavy stone used for grinding grain. He had no concept of glaciers, ice sheets, or continental slabs of ice collapsing into the ocean. Today, global warming is causing exactly that: enormous masses of ice breaking off and plunging into the sea. The ancient symbol aligns with the modern physical reality.

Babylon as a Worldwide System

In Revelation, Babylon is not a single city. It is a worldwide way of life, a global system built on commerce, greed, and idolatry. Babylon represents a civilization that believes it cannot fall because it is wealthy, connected, and powerful. It blinds people with luxury and distraction, convincing them that everything is stable even as it quietly drains the world around it.

Babylon is portrayed as a marketplace overflowing with goods, a culture intoxicated by wealth, and a world order built on exploitation. It collapses suddenly, not because of an external attack, but because the system destroys itself from within. This pattern mirrors the modern world almost exactly. The global system today is built on commerce that never stops, greed that never slows, and idolatry that elevates comfort, entertainment, and wealth above truth.

What Global Warming Actually Is

Global warming is not just hotter weather. It is the breakdown of the planet’s life‑support system — the ecosystem and climate engine that make civilization possible.

The World Ecosystem

The world ecosystem is a single interconnected network. Forests create rain, oceans absorb heat, ice reflects sunlight, soil holds nutrients, rivers carry minerals, and seasons guide growth. When one part collapses, the shock spreads through the entire system.

The Climate System

The climate system is the machinery that keeps the planet stable. Ocean currents move heat, jet streams guide weather, monsoons deliver rainfall, and storm tracks shift energy. Civilization depends on this stability.

How Far Gone the System Already Is

Many parts of the world system are already past their limits. Rainforests are drying, coral reefs have collapsed, permafrost is melting, ice sheets are destabilizing, ocean currents are weakening, storms are becoming erratic, and seasons are shifting.

Tipping Points and Cascades

A tipping point is a moment when a system becomes so stressed that it changes suddenly and permanently. When one tipping point is crossed, it pushes others forward, creating a cascade — a chain reaction of failures.

Rate‑Driven Amplification

Scientists have found that the rate of warming exacerbates and amplifies every tipping point. Faster warming weakens forests, reduces rainfall, increases heat, melts ice faster, and destabilizes weather. Each step amplifies the next.

Why Fast Warming Guarantees Exponential Collapse

When the rate of warming amplifies the next change, the curve cannot stay linear. It must bend upward. This is what exponential means: the speed of the process becomes part of the process.

Earth’s Venusification

Venus is an example of runaway feedback. Its average temperature is about 867°F (464°C). The atmosphere is thick carbon dioxide with clouds of sulfuric acid. Volcanic activity is widespread, and surface pressure is over 90 times Earth’s.

Scientists believe Venus may once have had conditions suitable for liquid water. Whether it supported life is irrelevant. Venus stands as a signpost of what happens when a planet crosses too many tipping points too quickly.

Zombie Trees

Across the Amazon, the Congo, and Southeast Asia, many trees have entered a comatose state. During recent heat‑dome events — where high‑pressure air traps extreme heat over a region — the canopy was flash‑fried. Leaves stayed green, but internal structures collapsed.

These trees are not photosynthesizing normally, not producing rainfall, not regulating humidity, and not storing carbon. They use just enough energy to keep their leaves green. Satellites detect greenness, but greenness is not life.

Why the Forests Were Misdiagnosed

Satellites measure reflectance, not function. After heat‑dome flash‑fry events, leaves remained green even though the trees were no longer active. Ground reports revealed forests that looked intact from above but were ecologically dead.

The Self‑Destruction Sequence

The world’s tipping points collapse in a sequence. Each failure exacerbates and amplifies the next. Drying forests weaken rainfall, weak rainfall destabilizes soil, reduced plant growth increases heat, increased heat melts ice faster, and disrupted currents destabilize weather.

This sequence is self‑sustaining. Once triggered, it becomes impossible to stop. The system begins feeding on itself.

Why Climate Timelines Keep Moving Closer

Early climate models assumed linear warming. Real‑world data shows accelerating change. Ice melts faster than predicted, forests dry sooner, permafrost thaws deeper, and currents weaken more rapidly. The system is destabilizing faster than early models assumed.

What Happens If the Ice Falls Into the Sea

If the Antarctic and Greenland ice sheets collapse, sea levels rise by tens of meters. Coastlines disappear, nations lose land, islands vanish, currents break down, weather becomes chaotic, food systems fail, and governments destabilize.

“Every Island Fled Away” — Revelation 16:20

John described islands disappearing. Today we understand the cause: massive sea‑level rise from collapsing ice sheets.

Babylon and the Modern World

Revelation’s Babylon is a global system built on commerce, greed, and idolatry. It collapses suddenly and cannot be rebuilt. The modern world is crossing tipping points and cannot return to stability.

The Millstone and the Glacier

John saw a massive object thrown into the sea. Today, massive glaciers collapse into the ocean, destabilizing the planet and ending the global system built on excess.

Final Takeaway

Global warming is not just hotter weather. It is the collapse of the world system — the ecosystem, the climate engine, and the civilization built on them.

Summary — The Tipping Points Working Together

The tipping points are already active, reinforcing one another, and driving the world system toward collapse. The end is not approaching — it is unfolding.
